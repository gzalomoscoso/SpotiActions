Made to be looked at

Widgets that belong on your wallpaper, not on top of it.

Every widget has one switch: draw its own card, or let your wallpaper through. Light and dark follow your phone. Real data from your calendar, reminders, mail, steps and the sky — read on the device, shown on the Home Screen.

Transparent, by one switch

Long-press a widget, tap Edit, turn on Transparent. The card disappears and the text, charts and icons sit straight on your wallpaper.

Light and dark, mirrored

Each widget has a day palette and a night palette that keep the same hierarchy with the lightness turned over — never an inverted mess.

Nothing leaves your phone

Calendars, reminders, steps, distance and mail are read on the device. No account, no analytics, no third-party SDKs. Mail passwords live in the Keychain.

Three steps

On your Home Screen in a minute.

1

Open the app once

It asks for the permissions the widgets need — calendar, reminders, motion, Health, location for the weather — one at a time. Each one only feeds its own widget.

2

Add a widget

Touch and hold the Home Screen, tap Edit → Add Widget, search for DataFastView and pick the one you want.

3

Make it transparent

Touch and hold the widget, tap Edit Widget, turn on Transparent. Your wallpaper shows through.

And the rest of the app

Still the toolkit it always was.

  • Status — free space, link quality and a real speed test: download, upload, latency and jitter, with charts.
  • Dynamic Island and Lock Screen — fourteen live metrics, or a phrase of your own, in the colour you pick.
  • Control Center — your shortcuts one swipe away.
  • Track — your day on a map: where you stopped and for how long. Kept on your device for 90 days.
  • Passwords — a generator for strong passwords, ready to paste.
Private by design

Read on the device. Shown on the device.

  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no third-party SDKs.
  • Mail widgets connect straight to your mail server over TLS; credentials stay in the Keychain.
  • Health is read-only, for walking and running distance, and only ever displayed.
  • Weather comes from Apple Weather, with attribution on the widgets.
